SHEET PAN
NACHOS.

Maximum chip to topping ratio achieved by spreading everything on one tray.

A small snack still deserves attention. Let Monterey Jack or cheddar carry the bite, use black beans for contrast, and add the salt, heat, or acid while the food is at its best.

Every chip gets a turn

Spread the chips in one layer before you add anything. A small amount of topping on every chip is better than a mountain of cheese in the middle. Bake until the cheese is bubbling and the exposed edges have darkened, then add sour cream and salsa after the tray comes out. The cold toppings are what keep the nachos from eating like one soft mass.

Nachos are usually framed as food for a crowd, which makes a solo tray look like a compromise. It is not. The small format gives every chip the same chance at cheese, beans, and jalapeno. A lot of women-targeted food content asks people to make their meals decorative. This one asks only for good coverage.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Layer

    Spread 2 large handfuls of tortilla chips in a single layer on a baking sheet. Scatter 1/4 can of drained black beans, 2 tablespoons of pickled jalapenos, and 80g of shredded cheese over every chip.

  2. 02

    Bake

    Bake at 400°F (200°C) for 8 to 12 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling and the exposed edges of the chips have deepened in color.

  3. 03

    Top and eat

    Add cold sour cream and salsa. Eat directly from the tray.

Easy swaps

Missing Monterey Jack or cheddar? Any sturdy cracker-worthy substitute works. The point is contrast between bites, not exact replication.

Storage

This is best straight away. If Monterey Jack or cheddar or black beans needs to wait, cover it separately for one day and reassemble so the crisp parts do not soften.

Slop Notes

Eat straight from the sheet pan. It keeps them crispy and saves a dish.
