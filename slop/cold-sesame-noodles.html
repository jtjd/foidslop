COLD SESAME
NOODLES.

Room temperature noodles in a nutty, gingery sauce. Better cold than hot.

Rinse the cooked noodles until they are genuinely cool, then drain them very well so water does not dilute the sesame sauce. Thin the sauce separately until it falls from a spoon in a ribbon before tossing.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Cook and cool

    Boil the 100g of noodles per package, then rinse under cold water until completely cool. Drain well.

  2. 02

    Sauce and toss

    Whisk the 2 tablespoons of tahini, 1 tablespoon of soy, 1 tablespoon of rice vinegar, 1 teaspoon of grated ginger, and 1 teaspoon of sesame oil. Thin with water until silky. Toss the cold noodles with the sauce. Top with shredded cucumber, spring onions, sesame seeds, and a swirl of chilli oil.

Easy swaps

Chinese sesame paste gives the deepest toasted flavor, but tahini or smooth peanut butter also works. Use rice noodles for a gluten-free bowl and tamari in place of soy sauce. Shredded carrot can replace cucumber.

Storage

Refrigerate dressed noodles in a sealed container for up to two days. The sauce thickens when cold, so loosen each serving with a teaspoon of water and toss again before eating.

Slop Notes

The sauce should look slightly loose before it meets the noodles. Chilled noodles absorb moisture quickly, and a stiff sauce will turn pasty instead of silky.
