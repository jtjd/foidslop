PANZANELLA.

Published June 30, 2026

Tuscan bread salad. Stale bread soaked in the juices of very ripe tomatoes.

Panzanella works because the ingredients keep their own texture. Cut day old sourdough into fork-friendly pieces, dress lightly, and let the sharper flavors settle for a minute.

Stale bread, good idea

Salt the tomatoes before you add the bread. Ten minutes gives them time to release the juice that becomes the dressing. Stale sourdough can take that moisture without collapsing. Toss gently, let the salad sit for another few minutes, and add the basil at the end. The bread should be softened in spots but still have a little chew.

Panzanella is a useful rebuttal to polished food content. Its best ingredient is bread that has been left around too long, and its dressing comes from tomato juice. The dish does not need a fresh-from-the-market fantasy to work. It needs ripe tomatoes, a little patience, and permission to use what is already there.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Salt tomatoes

    Season 2 chunked tomatoes generously with salt and let sit 10 minutes. They will release juices.

  2. 02

    Soak bread

    Toss 2 slices of torn sourdough, 1/4 thinly sliced red onion, 2 tablespoons of olive oil, and 1 tablespoon of red wine vinegar with the tomatoes. Let sit 5 to 10 minutes so the bread absorbs the juices.

  3. 03

    Finish

    Add a handful of fresh basil, toss, and serve at room temperature.

Easy swaps

Greens swap freely by season. Without day old sourdough, toasted nuts or seeds restore the crunch the salad is counting on.

Storage

The vegetables keep for two days when dry and covered. Hold the dressing apart, then add day old sourdough and taste again just before eating.

Slop Notes

This only works with stale bread and peak season tomatoes. Both requirements are non negotiable.
