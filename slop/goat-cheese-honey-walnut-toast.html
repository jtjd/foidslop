Tangy goat cheese on toasted bread with honey. Easy, satisfying, and kind of impressive.

A thick slice turns soft goat cheese and good honey into a proper one-person meal. Watch the color of the bread rather than the clock, then assemble while the surface still crackles.

Toast with a point

Bring the goat cheese to room temperature before spreading it. Cold chevre tears the toast and makes you press too hard. Toast the walnuts if they are raw, then add them after the honey so they stay crunchy. A few slices of pear or fig turn this into a more generous plate, while thyme keeps the sweet parts from taking over.

This is the kind of toast that gets photographed as a small luxury and then dismissed as decorative food. The useful part is the balance. Tangy cheese, crisp bread, honey, and toasted nuts cover a lot of ground in two slices. A pretty plate can still be a practical dinner for one.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Spread

    Spread 60g of soft goat cheese generously on the 2 slices of toasted sourdough.

  2. 02

    Top

    Drizzle with 1 tablespoon of good honey, scatter 2 tablespoons of toasted walnuts, and add fresh thyme if using.

Easy swaps

Out of soft goat cheese? Try good honey instead, or use any combination of something creamy over something briny. Thick bread forgives almost any topping swap.

Storage

Make the components ahead, not the finished toast. Cover soft goat cheese, protect good honey from moisture, and assemble after the bread is hot and crisp.

Slop Notes

Room temperature goat cheese spreads much more easily than cold. The combination of honey and goat cheese is very nearly perfect.
