True carbonara. No cream, just eggs, guanciale, and pecorino. Perfect.

Carbonara succeeds in the minute after the pasta leaves the water. Let the guanciale pan cool briefly, toss off the heat, and add reserved pasta water a spoonful at a time until the egg and cheese become glossy rather than scrambled.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Render guanciale

    Cook 50g of guanciale in a cold pan over medium heat until fat renders and edges crisp. Remove from heat.

  2. 02

    Make sauce

    Whisk 1 egg, 1 yolk, 30g of pecorino, and 20g of parmesan together with generous black pepper.

  3. 03

    Combine carefully

    Add hot pasta to the guanciale pan off heat. Add egg mixture, tossing rapidly, adding pasta water gradually until silky.

Easy swaps

Pancetta is the closest replacement for guanciale; bacon works but adds smoke. Use all Pecorino Romano for a sharper sauce or combine pecorino and parmesan for a gentler one. Spaghetti, bucatini, and rigatoni all work.

Storage

Carbonara is best eaten immediately. Refrigerate leftovers for up to one day, then warm very gently with a splash of water; high heat will tighten the egg sauce and make it grainy.

Slop Notes

If the pan sizzles when the egg mixture goes in, it is too hot. Toss the pasta for 20 to 30 seconds first, then add the eggs and loosen with hot pasta water.
