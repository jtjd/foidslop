Noodle Recipes for One

Noodles are the fastest route from hungry to eating, but the method changes with the bowl. Cold sesame noodles need a thorough rinse and a loose sauce; ramen needs concentrated broth and strong toppings; soba and udon need careful timing. Every recipe here is portioned for one and explains the cue that keeps the noodles from turning soft or sticky.

A better starting point

Noodle night rules

  • Read the package time, then taste one noodle a minute early; carryover heat finishes hot bowls quickly.
  • Reserve noodle water for warm sauces, but rinse noodles thoroughly when the finished dish is meant to be cold.
  • Mix sesame, peanut, or chilli sauces before the noodles are ready so every strand gets coated immediately.
  • Add crunchy toppings last: chilli crisp, scallions, sesame seeds, peanuts, or crisp vegetables.

Noodle night questions

What noodles work best?

Whatever cooks fastest and holds sauce: instant ramen, wheat noodles, soba, and rice noodles all appear across these recipes.

Are instant noodles fair game?

Completely. Two recipes here start with them. The upgrades take less time than delivery takes to arrive.

How do I keep noodles from clumping?

Use enough water, stir during the first minute of cooking, rinse noodles going into cold preparations, and dress hot noodles immediately.

How do I make instant ramen taste better?

Use a little less water, add an egg or tofu for substance, and finish off the heat with butter, sesame oil, miso, chilli crisp, or another aromatic ingredient.
